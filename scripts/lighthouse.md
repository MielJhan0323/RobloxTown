# The Lighthouse for Lost People

Rule: In this town, the lighthouse shines for anyone who's lost.
Music: piano
Voice: ElevenLabs "Anatoly" (UgMQ92GJ3C3bUAtHUKyd), eleven_v4

## Narration

In this town, the lighthouse shines for anyone who's lost. Not just ships. People.

When Tommy Briggs got lost at the summer fair, the beam found him at the cotton candy stand. When Mrs. Alder lost her glasses, it lit up her own head. They were on it. And when my dad lost his job, it shone on our porch for a whole week. He pretended not to notice. He noticed.

Then, we moved. Across town, to a little blue house by the docks. My sister June was six. She stopped talking the day we packed up her room. Not sad-quiet. Just gone-quiet. Mom tried pancakes. Dad tried knock-knock jokes. I gave her my bike. Nothing.

The first foggy night, I couldn't sleep. I looked out my window, and the beam was sweeping the whole town. Then it stopped. Right on our house. On June's window.

I tiptoed into her room. She was sitting up, staring at the light. "Are you lost?" I whispered. She nodded. "Me too, a little," I said. "New house. New street. New everything." The light stayed all night. So did I.

The next morning, the lighthouse keeper, old Mr. Pell, knocked on our door with a jar of sea glass. "The light doesn't fix lost," he said. "It just shows someone where to sit." June took the jar. She held a blue piece up to the sun. And for the first time in three weeks, she said one word. "Home."
