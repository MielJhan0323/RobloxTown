## Narration

In this town, everyone has a meter on their wrist that shows how much time they have left.

Kids compare them at recess like trading cards. My grandpa's says twenty years, and he still won't eat his vegetables. My uncle checks his more than his phone. And if you really love someone, you can give them your time. But only once.

Then, I woke up in a hospital. I didn't remember the accident. I didn't remember much of anything. My meter said sixty-one years.

The girl across the hall visited me every day. Her name was Lily. She brought pudding cups and the worst jokes I'd ever heard. She always wore a bracelet over her meter. We played cards until the nurses kicked her out. She always let me win. But every day, Lily looked a little more tired. First, she stopped walking over. Then, she came in a wheelchair. Then, her jokes got quieter. One day, she handed me a grape pudding before I even asked. "How did you know grape is my favorite?" I asked. She smiled. "Lucky guess."

The next morning, the doctor checked my chart. "Your meter hit zero in the ambulance," she said. "Someone gave you their time. They didn't leave a name." I couldn't stop thinking about it. The pudding she always knew. The bracelet she never took off. How she got weaker every day I got stronger. I walked across the hall. I slid her bracelet down. Her meter said one month.

And suddenly, I remembered. A treehouse. A girl with a gap-toothed grin. Two grape puddings, always split in half. "It was you," I said. She looked down. "You always shared yours with me," she said. "So I shared mine."

In this town, you can only give your time away once. But nobody ever said you can't give it back. I held my wrist against hers. The numbers flickered. Hers went up. Mine went down. Until they stopped. Thirty years. Exactly the same. Lily laughed and cried at the same time. "Now what?" she whispered. I split the grape pudding in half. "Now we share."

digital watch face. Lily's is hidden under a beaded bracelet until shot 41.

- **stage 1** (shots 15-21): cheerful, bright clothes, walking, big smiles.

- **stage 4** (58-61, after the time is shared): sitting up, bright smile, warm light again.

Frame faces in the upper 40% of the 9:16 frame (captions sit on the chest). Mouse out of view when recording.
